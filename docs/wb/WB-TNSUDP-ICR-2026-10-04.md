# The ICR twist: the same system, two completion stories — 2026-10-04

Follow-up to `WB-ADDENDUM-2026-10-04.md`. Three more Bank documents pulled and archived here:
`TNSUDP_ICR_2023-09-22.pdf` (P150395 Implementation Completion & Results Report), `TNSUDP_ICR-review_2024-02-20.pdf` (IEG review), `TNSUDP_PP_2022-2023.pdf` (final TNSUDP procurement plan, Mar 2023 — zero flood/CRTFF lines, archived for completeness).

## 1. The Bank's own completion report claims our flagship as DONE

The TNSUDP (Tamil Nadu Sustainable Urban Development Program, P150395, closed Sep 2023) ICR states — verbatim:

> "The Real Time Flood Forecast System (RTFFS) for Chennai **developed under the project** allows the sharing of real-time data and the issuing alerts to various stakeholders based on rainfall predictions three days in advance. Information from the system is being used by the Water Resource Department to monitor lake operations and river flows during rains, by the Revenue Department to aid in flood relief measures, by the Police Department and the Fire Department to arrange flood relief and rescue works in flood-affected areas, and by the Education Department to plan school schedules during flood affected days. There is also a public-facing application to share and crowd-source inundation information from the public."

The results table lists **"6. RTFFS developed in GCC"** as an achieved output, and the independent IEG review (Feb 2024) repeats it: **"Installed Real-Time Flood Forecast System (RTFFS) for Chennai, as targeted."**

## 2. The number matches our sanction

The ICR's updated economic analysis: "The **INR 100 crore (US$12.08 million) investment in RTFFS** is therefore considered cost-effective, representing 0.3 percent of the average damage caused by floods" — benchmarked against ADB's 2021 Chennai–Kosasthalaiyar Basin damage estimate (₹26,700 crore). ₹100 crore ≈ the **₹107.2 crore GoTN sanction (2022)** documented in §5.1 of the report — the same money the portal credits as "Funded by World Bank" under PDGF.

## 3. The structural contradiction (three financing identities, one flagship)

| Identity | Instrument | What the paper says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TNSUDP P150395, Component 2 (TNUIFSL-implemented) | IBRD loan closed Sep 2023 | ICR: RTFFS "**developed under the project**"; ₹100 crore invested; IEG: "installed ... as targeted" |
| PDGF (TNUIFSL) | GoTN sanction 2022, ₹107.2 crore | Report §5.1 sanction trail; portal credit "Funded by World Bank"; ICR/IEG documents contain **zero "PDGF" mentions** |
| TNCRUDP P179189 (TNUIFSL-implemented) | IBRD loan effective 2024 | Procurement plans Dec-23/Dec-24: RTDAS + control rooms, **US$6.06M "Pending Implementation", $0.00 disbursed**; line dropped in Aug-2026 plan |

The Bank recorded the flagship as **completed in Sep 2023** (ICR) while simultaneously listing its sensor-and-control-rooms package as **not yet tendered in 2024** (TNCRUDP plans), then dropping it in 2026. Both readings cannot be true of the same scope. Either (a) the ICR booked a system completed with TNSUDP/PDGF money whose RTDAS scale-up was later re-funded separately, or (b) the ICR claimed a crown jewel whose core hardware had not been procured — with the ₹100 crore "investment" being the GoTN sanction, not disbursed TNSUDP loan money. Nothing public distinguishes (a) from (b).

Note where the GCC flood-risk-mitigation TA sits: **Component 2, "Investments in Urban Services", implemented by TNUIFSL** (completion cost US$462.81M, IBRD US$290.51M) — the same implementing agency that runs PDGF and the RTFF & SDSS procurement. Component 3 (e-governance/GIS, DMA, US$13.88M at completion) is a different line; the ₹100 crore figure cannot fit inside it.

## 4. Who really paid the vendor?

The Bank's paper never names SECON/JBA or any payment voucher. What it does establish:

- The **institutional pipe is single**: TNUIFSL — TNSUDP Component 2, PDGF, and TNCRUDP all route through it. Any vendor payment to SECON or JBA for the flagship would have been signed off by TNUIFSL (or GCC acting under its TA), regardless of which window wore the label.
- The **₹100 crore ICR figure ≈ the ₹107.2 crore GoTN sanction** — pointing to state-budget money (PDGF route) being what "the project" actually spent, i.e. the ICR may have claimed a state-funded sanction as a Bank-project output. This is precisely the kind of double-counting a payment-voucher RTI can prove or kill.
- The **US$6.06M RTDAS package ($0 disbursed)** can't be what paid any vendor — it never left the starting line before vanishing from the plan.

RTI targets, sharpened (§5.6): (1) TNUIFSL — payment vouchers, UCs and bank statements for SECON and JBA Consulting invoices against the 2022 sanction, with the **head of account / funding-window code on each voucher** (that single field answers "TNSUDP loan, PDGF, or plain GoTN budget"); (2) GoTN Finance — the sanction order's funding source annotation; (3) DMA — TNSUDP Component-2 ATR listing the GCC flood-TA subcomponent's actual expenditure.

## 5. Why this matters to the audit

- **The "world bank-funded" portal credit is not one fact but an umbrella** across at least three distinct funding identities (closed IBRD loan, PDGF, live IBRD loan) that the Bank's own documents use interchangeably — while results are claimed where money may not have moved.
- **A results-integrity question now attaches to the flagship**: the Bank's flagship-completion claim (ICR + IEG) predates any evidence of the hardware being operational (our freshness audit shows data only from 2026 dashboards; control rooms were "pending" in 2024). If the ICR is right, the system existed fully by Sep 2023 — then why was its RTDAS package re-procured from scratch under a new loan? If the procurement plans are right, the ICR claimed a phantom.
