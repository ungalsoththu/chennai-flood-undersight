                                Section 1.              Integrated Fiduciary Systems Assessment (IFSA)

1. 1     Fiduciary Risk Assessment – Conclusion


1. An Integrated Fiduciary Systems Assessment (IFSA) of the key selected implementing agencies has been
   carried out in accordance with Bank Policy and Directive for Program for Results (PforR) financing. This is a
   multi-sector (Urban Sector, Water Sector, and Governance Sector) and multi-agency program. The key
   implementing agencies are: Commissionerate/Directorate of Municipal Administration1 (CMA/DMA) which is
   a directorate under the Municipal Administration and Water Supply Department (MAWSD) and is a core
   government department, Tamil Nadu Urban Infrastructure Financial Services Limited (TNUIFSL) which is a
   state government company, and 21 Urban Local Bodies2 (ULBs) which are part of the third level of government
   as per the constitutional framework of the country.

2. The IFSA assess whether the fiduciary system and performance of the Program’s fiduciary system are
   adequate and provides reasonable assurance that the funds would be used for the intended purposes with
   due attention to the principles of economy, efficiency, effectiveness, transparency, and accountability. The
   IFSA is prepared based on the meetings and data/details provided by the officials of Implementing Agencies
   (IA). The review relies upon documents and data provided by the IA regarding their fiduciary processes and
   performance in past and upon the Bank’s past project experience and understanding of the Financial
   Management (FM) and Procurement systems at the State level.

3. IFSA is covering both the state level agencies and ULBs involved in the program on a sample basis. 3 ULBs
   namely Avadi, Salem, and Kanchipuram was selected as representative sample as all ULBs follow the same FM
   system and procurement system. These 2 ULBs were selected as they were among the ULBs who are expected
   to implement large sub projects and receive large portion of funding from this Program.

4. Based on the IFSA, given the experience of the main implementing agencies DMA and TNUFISL, the
   combined fiduciary risk is rated as “Moderate”. The risk at ULB level is higher due to varying capacity, and
   first time implementation of PFR. There is an inherent risk associated with variations in fiduciary capacity,
   compliance to agreed FM and procurement processes at ULB level. To mitigate fiduciary risk and strengthen
   the existing systems, various actions are recommended to be completed during preparation and
   implementation as Program Action Plan (PAP). With the implementation of recommended mitigation
   measures and system strengthening actions, during the program implementation, the capacity and
   performance of the Program implementing agencies is considered adequate to provide reasonable assurance
   that the financing proceeds would be used for the intended purposes, with due attention to the principles of
   economy, efficiency, effectiveness, transparency, and accountability. If any significant events and issues come

5. Government of Tamil Nadu (GoTN) has a good procurement and financial management (FM) framework
   which all implementing agencies follow. GoTN follows the Tamil Nadu Transparency in Tenders Act ,1998
   (TNTTA; the Tamil Nadu Transparency in Tenders Rules, 2000; and the Tamil Nadu Transparency in tenders
   (PPP procurement) Rules 2012 that broadly meets the core procurement principles. FM arrangements in the
   state are guided by The Tamil Nadu Finance Code Volume I and II, The Tamil Nadu Accounts Code Volume I
   and II, The Tamil Nadu Treasury Code Volume I and II, The Tamil Nadu Budget Manual Volume I and II, Tamil
   Nadu Public Works Department (PWD) Code and time to time government orders, are reasonably well
   established and followed. The existing governance and accountability arrangements include audits by the
   Comptroller and Auditor General (C&AG) of India, review of audit reports by Public Accounts Committee, Right
   to Information (RTI) Act 2005, and Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) Act 2003. The State departments and
   government agencies have clearly defined delegation of authority. Parastatals also have a clear delegation of
   powers, financial rules and regulations which are followed.

                Existence of Fixed Asset Register and physical verification of assets; Record keeping.

9. The project envisages contracts in Goods, Civil Works, Consultancy, and non-Consultancy, of various values.

   confirmed that there are no high-value contracts in the Program at this stage. In accordance with the Program

   a) Works, including turnkey, supply & installation of plant and equipment, and PPP): US$ 115 million
   b) Goods, including Information Technology or Non-consulting Services: US$ 75 million
   c) Consulting Services: US$ 30 million

10. The IAs shall submit report to the World Bank if any large contracts are envisaged throughout program
    implementation. Format will be annexed to the Program Operation Manual. In addition, the Bank will review
    the audit, the Program budget execution and contract management reports to identify any large‐value
    contracts to ensure conformity with the Bank’s policy on Program for Results (PforR).

                                       Section 2.         Scope and Program Boundary

2. 1    Implementation Arrangements

12. The Program’s institutional framework will rely on the state’s existing institutions that comprises a two-tier
    government system of state level and ULB level institutions. At the state level, the TNUIFSL and DMA will be
    the nodal implementing agencies. 21 ULBs will be implementing agencies at the ULB level. Refer Figure 1.

13. The DMA is responsible for implementing the urban governance improvement component of the Program.
    DMA will be responsible for coordinating and overseeing the achievement of Results Area 1 and will pass on
    the funds to ULBs as grants, upon achievement of the DLIs. DMA had previously functioned as the
    implementing agency for institutional capacity building activities under the World Bank supported urban
    development projects as well as other projects. It has also coordinated implementation of reforms at ULB and
    State level under AMRUT and SBM. It is also main implementing partner for SBM and in case of AMRUT, it
    passes on funds to TUFIDCO for onward transfers to the ULBs.

14. TNUIFSL will be responsible for Results Area 2 and will pass on the funds to ULBs as a mix of sub-loans and
    sub-grants as well as for achievement of DLIs. TNUIFSL / TNUDF has its own Board of Directors that oversees
    and approves sub-loans / sub-grants to ULBs as per their lending policies. TNUIFSL had previously functioned
    as the implementing agency for institutional capacity building activities as well as coordinating agency for sub
    investments done in the ULBs, under the World Bank supported urban development projects as well as other
    projects.

15. At the ULB level, Municipal Commissioners and Municipal Engineers will be responsible for implementing
    Program activities and achieving the required results, under the supervision and monitoring of state level
    agencies.

3.2.3 Procurement system analysis
67. GoTN's Procurement Framework: The Tamil Nadu Transparency in Tenders Act (TNTTA), 1998, Tamil Nadu
    Transparency in Tenders Rules (TNTTR), 2000, and Tamil Nadu Transparency in Tenders (Public Private
    Partnership Procurement) Rules, 2012, relevant amendments issued from time to time, and delegation of
    financial powers11 constitutes the State's procurement framework. Chapter VI-A of Tamil Nadu Transparency
    in Tender Rules 2000 provides for purchase preference applicable to Domestic small-scale industrial unit in
    case of low value less than INR 2.5 million (~US$ 30,487) for Works and INR 1 million (~US$ 12,195) for other
    procurement categories- and local purchases, and spot purchases of certain commodities from public sector
    institutions.
68. Procurement Manual: The MAWSD has prepared an Engineering Manual in 2000 through TNUDP – II for urban
    local bodies as a day-to-day reference, particularly for the works. This manual requires upgrading, including
    changes in procurement, and contracting approaches in the previous two decades, including life-cycle costing,
    PPP contractual arrangements, and guideline for climate-resilient and green procurement practices.
69. Procurement methods: Open Tendering is the preferred procurement method used widely by all the
    Departments, including TNUIFSL. Other methods of procurement are single sourcing and spot purchase. In
    addition, single-envelope and two-envelope bidding provisions and a multi-stage procurement method are
    available. It is mandatory for Tenders of estimated cost INR 20 million (~US$ 243,902) or less to have a
    minimum submission period of 15 days; above this threshold, the minimum period is 30 days.
70. Standard Tender documents (STD): GoTN has not prepared and disclosed a universal suite of Procurement
    documents/templates. In general, IAs have developed customised procurement documents aligned with the
    provisions of the TNTTA and TNTTR, and subsequent amendments. In these lines, the MAWSD prepared a
    customised tender document for the Works – admeasurement (item rate) contract. The ULBs are using this
    document. However, the MAWSD need to develop the standard procurement documents for design and build
    operate (DBO) / performance-based contracts that are unavailable.

   disbursement amounts against each indicator, and the protocols for their verification. Disbursements will be
   made against successful achievements of DLIs which will be verified by the IVA.

112. Disbursements will be made against DLI irrespective of the program expenditure incurred at the time of
   releasing the DLIs. It is important to note that the actual expenditure at Program completion would be

   reconciled with disbursements at the end of the Program. Any amount disbursed by the Bank more than the
   actual expenditures spent under the Bank Program will be refunded to the Bank.

113.   The key principles guiding the World Bank disbursement of DLIs are as follows:
       a. DMA/TNUIFSL will prepare technical reports to document the status of achievement of DLIs. The
           technical reports will be verified by an IVA, appointed as per terms of reference agreed with the World
           Bank. On validation of DLIs by an IVA, the PMU will communicate the achievement of DLIs and
           corresponding DLI values to the World Bank along with supporting documents.
       b. In case, the IA is unable to achieve the DLI target set out in any year, it will be rolled over for future
           years till such time the DLI target is achieved.
       c. In the case of non-scalable DLIs, the World Bank will disburse the DLI value only upon full achievement
           of the DLI target.
       d. In the case of scalable DLIs, if partially achieved, the World Bank may authorize withdrawal of an
           amount lesser than the DLI value allocated to the said DLI target. The remaining amount of the DLI
           will be disbursed once the DLI target value is fully achieved.
       e. In case the IA is unable to achieve the DLI target (scalable or non-scalable), the World Bank may
           reallocate the proceeds of the Loan to another DLI, in consultation with the state.
       f. The World Bank will issue an official letter to the IA endorsing the achievement of the DLI target and
           value.
       g. The IA will submit the disbursement claim of the DLI value to the Controller Aid, Accounts and Audits
           (CAAA) Division of the Ministry of Finance in the GoI.
       h. The CAAA will submit the disbursement claim to the World Bank and the funds will be disbursed by
           the World Bank to the GoI under IBRD loan terms. The GoI will release funds to the state as per agreed
           financing norms between the Centre and the state.




114.   The procedure for claiming the Disbursement linked results are as follows:

       a. Step 1: Once the results are achieved, the agency provides a letter/mail to IVA stating that the results
          have been achieved along with the documentation for achievement of results.
       b. Step 2: IVA will conduct the verification as per the agreed protocol and prepare a DLR wise report
          stating their opinion on achievement and the amount of DLI which could be released.
       c. Step 3: Submit the report along with Disbursement Linked Results (DLR) achievement letter to the
          Bank Task team leader (TTL) for review and confirmation.
       d. Step 4: Once the TTL reviews and confirms the achievement of the DLR, a formal letter accepting the
          result and approval for withdrawal of disbursement will be issued to Department of Economic Affairs
          and the Program implementing agency.
       e. Step 5: On receipt of the letter the Program implementing agency can prefer a withdrawal with the
          office of the CAAA.
       f. Step 6: CAAA will lodge a claim with the Bank loans disbursal office and payment would be processed
          by the Bank to the CAAA

                                                  Page 46 of 61
              IFSA – Tamil Nadu Climate Resilient Urban Development Program (P179189)

       g. Step 7: CAAA on receipt of funds will work with the department of expenditure and ensure that funds
          are transferred to the state as Additional Central Assistance release. The fund would be transferred
          from GoI account to the GoTN account, and the process of disbursement would be completed.

115. In the last year of the Program, the IA in coordination with the World Bank, will reconcile the audited
   program expenditure with the DLI amounts disbursed by the World Bank. If the Borrower has failed to
   provide evidence satisfactory to the Bank that the disbursed amount does not exceed the total amount of
   Program Expenditures, the Bank will request a refund equivalent to such excess amount.

                                Annexure-1: ANTI-CORRUPTION GUIDELINES PROTOCOL

Pursuant to Section 5.13 of the General Conditions: (i) as defined and incorporated by reference in the Loan
Agreement for the financing of the “Tamil Nadu Climate Resilient Urban Development Program (TNCRUDP)” (the
“Program”), to be entered between India (the “Borrower”) and the International Bank for Reconstruction and
Development (the “Bank”); and also (ii) incorporated by reference in the Program Agreement for the Program, to
be entered between the Bank and the GoTN (“Program Implementing Entity”), the Borrower and the Program
Implementing Entity shall ensure that the Program is carried out in accordance with the provisions of the Bank’s
“Guidelines on Preventing and Combating Fraud and Corruption in Program-for-Results Financing”, dated
February 1, 2012 and revised on July 10, 201523 (variously, the “Anti-Corruption Guidelines” or “ACG”). Set out
below are the supplementary protocols to be followed in implementing the Anti-Corruption Guidelines for the
Program.

1.         Borrower Arrangements.

       a) The office of the DMA and TNUIFSL will be the GoTN’s focal offices for facilitating coordination among
          relevant agencies and assessing the implementation of such ongoing actions by those agencies to ensure
          that the Program activities are implemented in accordance with the provisions of the ACG and these
          protocols.

       b) As there is no distinction between Bank-financed activities and government-financed activities within the
          Program, the ACG shall be applied in an unrestricted manner on all activities within the Program boundary.

2.         Reporting.

       a) For purposes of Section 6 of the ACG, DMA and TNUIFSL will collect, consolidate and provide the Bank,
          on a half yearly basis, with a report on: (1) any material and credible allegations and other indications of
          fraud and corruption under the Program that come to the attention of during such period, including those
          reported under the Whistle Blowers Protection Act (2014); (2) any investigations into such allegations;
          and (3) the progress of and outcomes from such investigations and any remedial or corrective actions
          taken or planned in response to such allegations or the findings of such investigations.

       b) It will also report therein that none of the contract awards under the Program are made to any of the
          Bank debarred or suspended persons or entities.

       c) A template for reporting under paragraph 2. (a) above is attached to this Protocol as Appendix-A.

       d) Where such allegations might constitute a serious risk to the implementation of the Program, the
          reputation of the Borrower or the Bank, or to the proper use of the proceeds of the Loan, DMA and
          TNUIFSL will immediately inform the Bank.

     e) For purposes of Section 7(a) of the ACG, the Bank will, consistent with its policies and procedures, inform

3.       Conduct of Investigations and Inquiries.

     a) For purpose of Section 6(d) of the ACG, DMA and TNUIFSL will ensure the timely and appropriate action
        to get investigation conducted on allegation and indications of fraud and corruption; and inform the Bank
        of its progress and findings at quarterly intervals and no later than one month after the conclusion of any
        investigation,

     b) For purposes of Sections 6(f) and 7(b) of the ACG, in the event that the Bank decides to conduct its own
        inquiry into allegations or other indications of fraud and corruption in connection with the Program, the
        Borrower, the Program Implementing Entity, and other entities implementing the Program will cooperate
        fully with the Bank’s representatives and take all appropriate measures to ensure the full cooperation of
        relevant persons and entities, including in each case, allowing the Bank to access sites and facilities, meet
        with relevant persons and/or entities, and to inspect all of their relevant accounts, records, and other
        documents and have them audited by, or on behalf of, the Bank.

     c) Any inquiries undertaken by the Bank are administrative in nature. The Bank’s right to investigate or
        conduct review does not extend to criminal investigations, which is the exclusive jurisdiction of the
        Borrower. The World Bank may, however, sanction any individual or entity other than the Member
        Country (as defined in the ACG) on its own pursuant to its prevailing policies and procedures.

     4. Suspension and Debarment.

     a) The Borrower and the Program Implementation Entity will ensure that any person or entity debarred or
        suspended by the Bank is not awarded a contract under, or otherwise allowed to participate in or benefit
        from, the Program during the period of such debarment or suspension by the Bank. To that end, the
        Borrower and the Program Implementation Entity will put in place appropriate measures, including but
        not limited to the following:

         (i)      Bidding documents will include information regarding the applicability of the ACG to the Program,
                  and exclusion of any debarred or suspended person or entity from a contract under or
                  participation in the Program.

         (ii)     the Bank’s listing of debarred or suspended persons or entities will be incorporated in the filter
                  used by implementing agencies when they conduct due diligence.

         (iii)    each participating bidder will be required to submit a self-declaration that the firm is not subject
                  to ineligibility or has not been sanctioned under the Bank system of debarment and cross-
                  debarment.
