1. INTRODUCTION
       The Revenue and Disaster Management

Department is one of the oldest Departments of

the Government of Tamil Nadu and occupies

a unique position in the administrative framework

of the State. Revenue Administration, including

District Administration, forms the backbone of

governance and serves as the primary interface

between the Government and citizens in matters

relating to land administration, maintenance of

land   records,   delivery   of   revenue   services,

implementation of Government schemes, public

grievance redressal, disaster management and

law and order support functions.

      The    Department             functions      through      an

extensive administrative network extending from

the State Headquarters to the village level and

plays a vital role in ensuring effective governance,

responsive administration and timely delivery of

public services across the State.

      The Government is committed to strengthen

Revenue     Administration           through        institutional

reforms,         technology-enabled                governance,

citizen-centric       service        delivery,      sustainable

infrastructure    and     capacity        building       of   field

(Reduced    Inequalities),          SDG    11      (Sustainable

Cities and Communities), SDG 13 (Climate Action)

and   SDG        16    (Peace,       Justice       and    Strong

Institutions).

2.2 District Administration

District Level
      At the district level, each of the 38 districts

is headed by a District Collector, assisted by the

District Revenue Officer and other district-level

officers. The District Collector is responsible for

coordinating Revenue Administration, maintaining

law and order, overseeing disaster preparedness,

response and relief, implementing welfare and

development programmes, conducting elections,

and ensuring effective grievance redressal. The

District Collector also coordinates the activities of

Government departments within the district and

monitors the delivery of essential public services.

Divisional Level

      At the divisional level, 94 Revenue Divisions

are   headed     by   Revenue   Divisional    Officers

or    Sub-Collectors.    They     supervise     Taluk


                         8
                         8
administration,         exercise         statutory          and

quasi-judicial      powers,        coordinate       field-level

activities    and   review     the    implementation         of

Government           programmes             within         their

jurisdictions.

Taluk Level

        At the Taluk level, 317 Taluks are headed by

Tahsildars.      They    maintain     and     update       land

records, collect land revenue and Government

dues,     issue      revenue        certificates,      protect

Government lands, implement social security and

welfare       schemes,        undertake      disaster-relief

functions and redress public grievances.

Firka Level

        At the Firka level, 1,247 Firkas are under

the charge of Revenue Inspectors. They supervise

Village      Administrative    Officers,     conduct       field

inspections      and     verification,     assist     in    the


                               9
                               9
protection of Government lands and prevention of

encroachments and support the implementation

of Government welfare programmes. They play an

important role in recovery of Government dues

under Revenue Recovery Act.

Village Level

         At the village level, 16,770 Revenue Villages

are served by Village Administrative Officers and

Village      Assistants.       They      constitute   the

Government's       frontline     field    machinery   for

maintaining village accounts, collecting revenue,

reporting local conditions and disasters, verifying

beneficiaries, assisting citizens and supporting the

delivery of Government services at the grassroots

level.

2.9 Adangal

       Adangal       is    the       basic    land   register

maintained by the Village Administrative Officer

for each village. This register contains details of

land   classification,      extent,     assessment,       crops

cultivated and irrigation source. Adangal register

entries     are   used     to    assess      the   food   crop

production and also to provide relief to crop

damage caused by flood and drought.

2.11.6 Online Data purity exercise prior to

applications, an automated process of data purity

exercise and real time sanctioning of approved

and data verified beneficiaries to the existing

vacancies in each district will be implemented

during the financial year.

         During processing of applications received

online, the VAO and Revenue Inspector enquire



                          30
                          30

Civil   Registration      System,          Labour       pension,

Revenue Administration, as per their seniority as

against      the      beneficiary          vacancy        arising

periodically.

     A sum of Rs.20,000 is being paid as relief to

the Legal heirs of the deceased primary bread

the age group of 18-59 years who were engaged

in the 44 notified categories of employment.

A sum of Rs.7,500 to Rs.15,000 is being paid to

the primary bread winner of the family depending

upon the nature of injury sustained and the

degree of physical impairment. This scheme is

2.13 Distress Relief Scheme

      A sum of Rs.20,000 is being paid as relief to

the legal heirs of the deceased primary bread

winner of the Below poverty line family (male or

female) while he or she is in the age group of 18

to 59 years.    This scheme is implemented with

100% funds from the Central Government.

3. DISASTER MANAGEMENT

       Preparedness and resilience form the two

main     components      of     disaster         management.

Disaster    preparedness        saves      lives.   Resilience

must be built through sustained investments in

infrastructure and human capacity. Minimizing

losses     through    preparedness             measures    and

scientific investments, is central to Tamil Nadu’s

Disaster Management mission. Over the past two

decades, the State has progressively shifted from

a   relief-centric     approach           to     focused   on

preparedness,        disaster      risk        reduction   and

resilience building. Recurring disasters; emerging

climate risks; topographical vulnerabilities arising

from the State’s long coastline, relatively flat

terrain interspersed by river basins and bordered

on the West by the Ghats; rapid industrialisation

                              54
                              54
and urbanisation; all have chiselled the evolution

of this approach. Disaster management in the

State is prompted by risk-oriented planning,

institutional       strengthening,         technological

innovation and resilient development activities.

      Tamil       Nadu's    geographical        diversity,

climatic variability and industrial development

expose the State to a wide range of natural and

anthropogenic hazards. The State's multi-hazard

risk profile is as follows:-

a comprehensive framework for action. The State

a    risk-informed     development         perspective    by

embedding disaster risk reduction considerations

within     planning,      budgeting       and   governance

Better,     the    Plan     recognizes      river   basins,

all-hazards,      all-phases      and      all-stakeholders

the Disaster Management Act, all districts have

prepared    their    District     Disaster       Management

7.14 SETTLEMENT

      The procedure to determine the assessment

and the ownership was known as Land Revenue

Settlement.

      In 1802, the British Government introduced

the   Permanent        Settlement    and       in    the   year

1879-80, the Ryotwari Settlement system was



                           209
                           209
introduced to assess taxes based on productivity,

considering factors like soil type, irrigation and

classification. Under this system, farmers (ryots)

paid taxes directly to the Government. The

Government conducted surveys for the first time,

determined   classifications,   and   created   patta

registries to record ownership. This system lasted

for 30 years, after which Re-settlement was

implemented.

      By the time India gained Independence, the

Ryotwari Settlement had been implemented in

most areas of the former Madras Presidency,

except for Estates/Inam Estates/Minor Inam and

similar areas.
