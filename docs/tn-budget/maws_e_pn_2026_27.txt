             VISION STATEMENT

    To build resilient, inclusive, climate-smart and
future-ready   urban      local   bodies   by     ensuring
universal   access     to       safe   drinking     water,
sustainable sanitation, efficient Municipal services,
green   infrastructure,     digital    governance     and
citizen-centric administration, thereby enhancing
the quality of life, promoting equitable economic
growth and creating livable cities for all.




                            1
1. PREAMBLE
        Urbanization is rapidly redefining the global
economic landscape. Today, over 57% of the
world’s population lives in urban areas and by
2050, nearly seven out of every ten people are
expected        to        reside   in    cities.     Globally,   cities
generate around 80% of economic output, making
them     the         principal      engines          of    innovation,
productivity and sustainable development.

        Consequently, leading nations are shifting
their    focus            from     merely      expanding         urban
infrastructure to building smart, compact, climate-
resilient,   carbon-neutral              and       digitally   enabled
cities that enhance livability while addressing the
challenges of climate change.

India Urbanization

        India        is    undergoing        one      of   the   most
significant urban transitions in the world. Although
only about 36% of the population currently lives


                                     2


                                     2
in urban areas, cities contribute nearly two-thirds
of the nation's GDP and this share will continue to
grow as urbanisation accelerates. By 2050, nearly
half of India's population is expected to live in
cities, placing unprecedented demands on urban
infrastructure, housing, mobility, water security,
environmental     sustainability          and    Municipal
governance.     The   national     urban        agenda     is
therefore evolving from expanding infrastructure
to creating resilient, inclusive and economically
competitive cities.

Tamil Nadu Urbanization

       Tamil Nadu is uniquely positioned to lead
this   transformation.    With    nearly 55%        of its
population already residing in urban areas—well
above    the   national   average—the           State    has
emerged as one of India's most urbanized and
economically    dynamic        regions.    Urban        areas
contribute more than 70% of the Gross State
Domestic Product (GSDP), reflecting the pivotal

                           3


                           3
role of cities in driving industrialization, innovation
and employment.

      Unlike    many      states    where     growth     is
concentrated in a single metropolitan city, Tamil
Nadu has developed a balanced, polycentric urban
system anchored by multiple thriving cities that
collectively foster regional development. The next
phase of urban development must therefore move
beyond       physical     infrastructure     to       urban
transformation—creating cities that are livable,
resilient,   sustainable,      financially   empowered,
technology-driven        and       citizen-centric.     By
embracing innovation, strengthening Municipal
institutions and investing in high-quality urban
services, Tamil Nadu can position itself as India's
model for next-generation urban development
and    a     global     benchmark      for   sustainable
urbanization.

                            4
Policy   Direction:                    Reimagining          Urban
Tamil Nadu
      The next generation of urban development
in Tamil Nadu must move beyond the traditional
approach        of     constructing           infrastructure      to
delivering      measurable              improvements        in   the
quality    of        urban      life.     The     State's     urban
transformation should be driven by outcome-
oriented      planning,         where         every   investment
creates      resilient,      inclusive,         sustainable      and
economically vibrant cities.

      Urban          planning      must       integrate     climate
resilience,          disaster          risk     reduction        and
environmental sustainability to prepare cities for
future     challenges.          Balanced        development       of
Tier-II and Tier-III cities should be accelerated to
promote regional prosperity, reduce pressure on
metropolitan areas and create new engines of
economic growth.



                                   5


                                   5
        At the same time, Urban Local Bodies must
be   empowered         through          stronger     institutions,
professional        Municipal            management            and
innovative        financing           mechanisms—including
Municipal Bonds, land value capture and public-
private partnerships—to reduce dependence on
Government          grants        and     ensure       long-term
financial sustainability.

        Leveraging         digital    technologies,       artificial
intelligence, GIS-based planning and data-driven
governance          will         enhance           transparency,
accountability and service efficiency.

        Above all, urban development must remain
citizen-centric,     ensuring           universal    access      to
quality     infrastructure,           safe    public      spaces,
affordable     housing,          sustainable    mobility       and
reliable    basic    services,        thereby       transforming
Tamil      Nadu     into     a    benchmark         for    livable,
inclusive, resilient and globally competitive cities.

2.4 Implementation of the Tamil Nadu Urban
    Local Bodies Act, 1998 and the Tamil
    Nadu Urban Local Bodies Rules, 2023
        The Tamil Nadu Urban Local Bodies Act,
1998 has been revived and amended suitably by
the Tamil Nadu Urban Local Bodies (Amendment)

2.7.7 Flood Mitigation Works

      Urban coastal areas are more prone to
flooding than rural coastal areas due to their high
population       density,         extensive       built-up
infrastructure and reduced natural drainage. The
concentration     of   critical     infrastructure    and
economic activities in urban areas increases the
potential for damage and disruption during flood
events. In contrast, rural coastal areas generally
have lower population densities and larger natural
open spaces that help absorb and store excess
rainwater, thereby reducing flood impacts. Hence
it is necessary to manage the urban floods with
proper drainage system.

      Major     flood-prone       areas   are    Chennai,
Tiruvallur,        Chengalpattu,                Cuddalore,

4.        DIRECTORATE    OF                      TOWN
          PANCHAYATS (DTP)
        Town Panchayats form the foundation of
Tamil    Nadu’s   transitional     urban       governance
framework, administering emerging settlements
that     bridge     the   rural–urban          continuum.
Constituted under Article 243Q of the Constitution
as institutions of urban local self-Government for
transitional areas, Tamil Nadu pioneered the Town
Panchayat model and became the first State in
India to institutionalize this distinct category of
urban local body.

        Town Panchayats facilitate orderly urban
growth      by    extending       Municipal      services,
strengthening civic infrastructure and supporting
local economic development before settlements
evolve into Municipalities. They play a vital role in
promoting balanced regional development and
inclusive   urbanization,       particularly    in   taluk



                          126


                          126
headquarters,     commercial     Centres,        pilgrimage
destinations and industrial towns.

      Tamil   Nadu       has   479   Town      Panchayats
spread across 5,832.33 square kilometers and
comprising       7,429     wards.      Their     combined
population, recorded at 66.50 lakh in the 2011
Census, is presently estimated at approximately
76.50 lakh. They therefore constitute a significant
segment of the State’s urban landscape.

      Their primary responsibilities include the
provision and maintenance of safe drinking water,
sanitation, solid waste management, roads, street
lighting   and    other    essential     civic    services.
Through efficient service delivery and sustainable
infrastructure    development,       Town      Panchayats
seek to improve citizens’ quality of life while
fostering clean, healthy, resilient and well-planned
urban settlements.

5. CHENNAI METROPOLITAN WATER
   SUPPLY AND SEWERAGE BOARD
   (CMWSSB)
     The     Chennai    Metropolitan           Area   (CMA),
comprising    Chennai    City       and      the   contiguous
areas, was notified in the year 1974. In order to
cater to the growing demand for water supply and
sewerage services in the Chennai Metropolitan
Area, the Chennai Metropolitan Water Supply and
Sewerage Board (CMWSSB) was constituted as a
statutory    body    under     the     provisions     of   the
Tamil Nadu Act 28 of 1978.

     The     Board     was         established     with    the
objective    of   ensuring         planned     development,
regulation, operation and maintenance of water
supply and sewerage services in the Chennai
Metropolitan Area. It is also entrusted with the
responsibility of protecting public health through
the provision of safe water supply and efficient



                             188


                         188
sewerage    management           and   with     all   matters
incidental thereto.

      Over the years, in addition to its core
functions, the Board has adopted information
technology–enabled initiatives to improve service
delivery.   Accordingly,      various         citizen-centric
online services have been introduced to facilitate
efficient, transparent and time-bound delivery of
services.

      The   Board     is   headed       by      a     full-time
Managing Director, who is responsible for its
overall administration.

      CMWSSB serves an estimated population of
approximately    91    lakh       across      an      area   of
426 square kilometers within Chennai City. The
Board delivers these services through a well-
established administrative network comprising 15
Area Offices and 200 Depots. In addition to
serving the city, CMWSSB also caters to the water

                           189


                           189
supply and sewerage requirements of select urban
areas adjoining Chennai, as well as industrial
establishments        located        within    its     service
jurisdiction.

5.1 Water Supply Management
5.1.1 Sources of Water Supply
       To meet the ever-growing water demand,
the   Board   has   adopted      a   diversified   water
resource management approach by utilizing a
combination of surface water, groundwater and
desalination sources.
i. Surface Water Sources
       Surface water remains the primary source
of water supply for Chennai City. The Chennai
Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board

                           192


                           192
(CMWSSB) draws water from major reservoirs
and lakes located in and around Chennai, viz
Poondi, Puzhal, Cholavaram, Chembarambakkam
and Kannankottai–Thervoy kandigai. Further, city
supplies are supplemented through water from
Veeranam source in Cuddalore District and from
Kandaleru Reservoir under the Krishna Water
Supply Project.
ii. Desalination Plants

        Chennai does not have perennial water
source    and   its    water      availability     is    largely
dependent on monsoon. To ensure long-term
water security and to enhance drought resilience,
the Government has implemented desalination
projects as an important component of Chennai’s
water      resource         augmentation             strategy.

supplementing         the    city’s       drinking        water

      Plants
      Two    tertiary treatment reverse osmosis

from industries.

6. TAMIL NADU WATER SUPPLY AND
   DRAINAGE BOARD (TWAD)
        Tamil Nadu Water Supply and Drainage
Board (TWAD Board) is a statutory organization of
the Government of Tamil Nadu established in
1971,      specially     for      the       comprehensive
development, implementation and stewardship of
drinking water supply and sewerage infrastructure
across the rural and urban areas of the State,
excluding the Chennai Metropolitan Area.

        With over five decades of public service,
TWAD Board occupies a central role in securing
safe, adequate and dependable water supply for
the   people    of     Tamil     Nadu.     Its   operations
encompass      the     entire     spectrum       of      water
management,      from     source        identification    and
scheme formulation to construction, water quality
surveillance     and       sustained         maintenance,
underpinned by a well-structured organizational



                           240


                           240
framework spanning the length and breadth of the
State.

      As the nodal bulk water supply agency of
the State, TWAD Board taps distant perennial
river sources and conveys treated water to rural
and urban local bodies through an expansive
Combined Water Supply Schemes (CWSS). Upon
commissioning, these schemes are operated and
maintained by the Board, ensuring continuity of
supply in both quantity and quality across all
beneficiary areas.

      Notwithstanding the substantial progress
achieved in expanding water supply coverage,
demand    continues    to     outpace    availability    in
several regions of the State, necessitating the
formulation   and    implementation       of   additional
schemes. In parallel, TWAD Board also addresses
the   water   requirements        of    industries      and
institutions, either through the existing CWSS


                            241


                        241
network        or   through        purpose-built    dedicated
supply schemes.

       Sustaining adequate water supply in Tamil
Nadu      is    a   major        challenge,   as   the   State
predominantly           relies     on   monsoon-dependent
surface water and groundwater sources, while
possessing only limited perennial river resources.
Compounding this constraint, a substantial portion
of the existing water supply infrastructure is
ageing,        giving     rise     to   pipeline    leakages,
transmission losses and service disruptions that
affect the reliability and regularity of supply to
beneficiaries.

       To address these challenges, TWAD Board is
actively implementing measures to curb Non-
Revenue Water (NRW) through the deployment of
IoT-enabled smart water meters integrated with a
Centralised         Water        Monitoring   System.     This
advanced digital infrastructure, unprecedented in
its scale and scope within the State, marks a

                                  242


                                  242
decisive transition towards data-driven, precision-
oriented water management.

      Beyond drinking water supply, TWAD Board
is implementing Underground Sewerage Schemes
in select urban local bodies to meet the growing
sanitation demands of expanding towns. The
Board is also pioneering the reuse of treated
wastewater       for     industrial       purposes,   with   a
dedicated    project       currently       under    execution.
Concurrently,     long-term            strategies   are   being
developed to institutionalize treated wastewater
recycling    at        scale,    progressively        reducing
dependence on freshwater sources and reinforcing
the commitment of the Government of Tamil
Nadu to sustainable water Management.

   At the field level, four Regional Offices of Chief
Engineers are situated at Vellore, Thanjavur,
Coimbatore and Madurai. Under these Regional
Offices,    16         Circle         Offices   headed       by
Superintending Engineers, 85 Divisional Offices

                                243


                                243
headed by Executive Engineers and 259 Sub-
Divisional Offices headed by Assistant Executive
Engineers collectively oversee the planning and
execution of water supply and sewerage schemes
across the State.

     Operate   and   maintain     Combined   Water
      Supply Schemes serving more than one

10.1.4 Operational Model and Key Challenges

      The water supply system operates on a
cross-subsidisation     model,       wherein   revenue
generated from industrial water supply is used for
domestic    water     supply    at    subsidized     rate.
However, due to Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD)
order issued by the Hon’ble High Court of Madras
in 2011, the revenue of the Company was
affected very badly as industrial water demand
fell below the projected level.

Way Forward
      Tamil Nadu has emerged as one of India's
leading urbanised States through balanced and
inclusive   urban development. As         urbanisation
continues to grow, the Government is committed
to creating cities and towns that are economically
vibrant,    environmentally      sustainable,   climate
resilient and citizen friendly. The focus will be on
ensuring that every Urban Local Body (ULB),
including Municipal Corporations, Municipalities
and Town Panchayats, has access to quality
infrastructure and efficient public services.

      The    Chief   Minister's    Integrated    Urban
Development      Mission     (CMIUDM)      2026–2031
provides the roadmap for the next phase of urban
development. With a proposed overall investment
of about Rs.1.5 lakh crore, the Mission brings
together major Central and State Government
schemes, institutional finance, Municipal bonds,


                           341


                           341
the    Urban      Challenge       Fund     and     multilateral
assistance          to       accelerate          infrastructure
development across the State.

       The Mission adopts an integrated approach
covering water supply, sewerage, storm water
drainage,      roads,       sustainable     urban      mobility,
affordable housing, restoration of water bodies,
urban greening, digital governance and climate-
resilient infrastructure. It aims to improve the
quality of life by providing reliable urban services,
strengthening        public      health,     protecting     the
environment and creating safe, inclusive and
livable cities.

       A     key      priority       of    the    Mission     is
strengthening the capacity of Urban Local Bodies
to    effectively    plan,     implement,        operate    and
maintain urban infrastructure. Continued reforms
in Municipal finance, revenue mobilisation, digital
governance,         asset    management          and    service
delivery will improve the financial sustainability

Municipal     officials   and        the   use    of   modern
technologies      will     further         enhance      project

        The Government will continue to promote

encourage innovative financing and strengthen
citizen participation to ensure transparent and
accountable        urban        governance.            Through

build resilient, inclusive and future-ready cities

quality of life and reinforce the State's position as
a      national   leader        in     sustainable       urban
