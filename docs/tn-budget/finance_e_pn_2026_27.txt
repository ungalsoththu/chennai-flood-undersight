Vision and Mission

      3. To maintain the fiscal health and
stability   of    the    State    through       prudent
financial   management,          prioritizing    public
welfare initiatives and critical infrastructure
projects    while       proactively    adapting      to
emerging economic challenges.               The core

    Budgetary Management: Preparing
      the Annual Budget in compliance with
      Article 202 of the Constitution of India
      and        adopting       modern    budgetary
      practices.

    Exchequer Stewardship: Managing
      public finances, balancing Government
      receipts and payments, and serving as
      the custodian of the State exchequer.

Role and Organisational Structure of
Finance Department

       5. The Finance Department is the nodal
department responsible for the management
of   the   State’s      public      finances.     As    the
custodian of the State Exchequer, it plays a
crucial role in preparing and presenting the
State Budget, scrutinizing financial proposals
of   administrative      departments,           regulating
expenditure,           managing                government
borrowing      and       debt        obligations,       and
maintaining         fiscal       discipline.     Financial
proposals     are     examined         regarding       their
necessity, cost-effectiveness, availability of
budgetary provision and conformity with
established financial procedures.
       The    Department            operates      through
several specialized wings, cells and units.
The principal components include:

Introduction
      7. Treasuries are among the oldest
operational units of Government. Originally
functioning as part of the Collectorates, they
were reorganised as a separate Department
on   01.04.1962      and      brought          under      the
administrative      control       of      the      Finance
Department. The Treasuries and Accounts
Department,       headed     by    the         Director    of
Treasuries    and    Accounts,           performs         the
State's    core     treasury       and          accounting
functions and also provides skilled financial
manpower      to    Departments,           Boards         and
Corporations.

      8.     The     present           field     structure
comprises 6 Regional Joint Director Offices, 6
Pay and Accounts Offices, 3 Sub Pay and
Accounts Offices, 38 District Treasuries, 1

    LOCAL FUND AUDIT DEPARTMENT

      50. The Local Fund Audit Department
traces its origin to the Madras Local Funds Act,
1871, which provided for audit of the accounts of
Local Boards. The post of Examiner of Local Fund
Accounts      was   created   in   1885   and    an
independent Local Fund Accounts Department
was formed in 1921. In 1995, the department
was renamed the Department of Local Fund
Audit and the Examiner was redesignated as the
Director of Local Fund Audit. Based on the
recommendations of the Second State Finance
Commission, the Government enacted the Tamil
Nadu Local Fund Audit Act, 2014 and the Local
Fund Audit Rules, 2016, providing the statutory
framework for audit of Local Bodies. The Director
General of Audit is the ex-officio Director of Local
Fund Audit.

        87. Under Section 80(1)(a) of the
Tamil Nadu Co-operative Societies Act, 1983,
read with Rules 101 and 102 of the Tamil
Nadu Co-operative Societies Rules, 1988, the
accounts       of   Co-operative    Societies     are

      88.     The     audit      process     comprises

with issue of the Audit Certificate and Audit
Report.     Statutory    and      compliance     audit
covers the financial statements, overdue
debts,     cash   and   securities,     valuation    of
assets     and    liabilities,    expenditure       and

Rules and by-laws and transactions involving
financial irregularities or impropriety.

and     valuation   of     assets    and     liabilities.

Tamil Nadu Green Climate Fund (TNGCF)

         126. Tamil Nadu Green Climate Fund
has been established to develop projects in
four     key   areas    viz.,   energy   transition,
electric    mobility,    circular   economy     and
food/water security. To accelerate these
investments in the State, the Environment,
Climate Change and Forests Department has
committed       Rs.100      crore   towards     the
establishment of the Tamil Nadu Green
Climate Fund (TNGCF), which has a target
corpus of Rs. 2,000 crore. The fund aims to

offset 25 million MT of carbon emissions and
enable the recycling of 2.5 million MT of
waste, contributing to the Government of
Tamil Nadu’s goal of achieving net-zero
emissions before 2070.
