Introduction

      Tamil Nadu is witnessing rapid urban
transformation, with its cities emerging as major
centres     of    economic        growth,     employment,
investment,         infrastructure           and      social
development. The pace of urbanization has also
led to significant migration from rural areas in
search      of   better      employment,        education,
healthcare and living conditions. While this
process contributes to economic development
and cultural diversity, it has increased pressure
on   housing,       transportation,         water    supply,
sanitation,      social     infrastructure     and    other
essential     urban       services.   Addressing      these
challenges requires an integrated approach that
combines effective land-use planning, affordable


                              1
housing,     efficient      mobility,         environmental
protection and adequate infrastructure.
        The Government’s vision is to transform
these      urban       centres        into      world-class
metropolitan       cities   through      planned         urban
growth,     sustainable       development,          modern
infrastructure and improved quality of life. The
Housing and Urban Development Department,
supported by its strong, well-established and
responsive     institutions       functioning    under     its
administrative control, such as Commissionerate
of Town and Country Planning (CTCP), Chennai
Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA),
Tamil Nadu Housing Board (TNHB), Tamil Nadu
Urban Habitat Development Board (TNUHDB),
Cooperative      Housing          Societies     (RCS(Hg.)),
Chennai Unified Metropolitan Transport Authority
(CUMTA) and Tamil Nadu Real Estate Regulatory
Authority (TNRERA)            plays a crucial role in
ensuring     planned,       balanced,        inclusive    and
sustainable development across the State.

   1. TAMIL NADU HOUSING BOARD
         The Tamil Nadu Housing Board (TNHB),
established under the Tamil Nadu State Housing
Board Act, 1961, has been at the forefront of
planned     urban      development         and     affordable
housing in the State for over six decades.
Evolving from the erstwhile City Improvement
Trust, TNHB has played a transformative role in
creating well-planned residential neighbourhoods
and integrated townships equipped with quality
infrastructure, thereby contributing significantly
to the

         TNHB   is    a   pioneering       institution   that
provides     dwelling     units     with     the    required
infrastructure, at an affordable cost. TNHB has
taken up Area Development schemes across the
state,    which      involves     the   development        of
planned layouts of plots and the construction of
houses/flats for all categories, viz. EWS, LIG,



                             4
MIG and HIG in a neighborhood development
concept with all amenities.
        In addition, TNHB had created the Board’s
rental flats for the general public and acts as a
nodal agency for constructing and maintaining
rental flats for Government servants at an
affordable rent under the TNGRHS category,
with Government grants. TNHB provides quality
construction by adopting modern construction
technologies such as pre-fabrication, Mivan and
Monolithic concrete and continues to develop
more neighbourhoods. The TNHB received the
award      “VISWAKARMA”         from        the         Union
Government for the construction of 1500 flats
using    new   prefabrication   technology             in    the
suburban area of Chennai, at Sholinganallur, in
2018.
1.1 Focus on Affordable Housing
        Recognizing    that   housing       is     a        basic
necessity, the Board has introduced innovative
housing    solutions   that   cater    to    all       income

groups.     TNHB’s     steadfast      commitment     to
delivering affordable housing is reflected in its
targeted efforts to support the Economically
Weaker Section (EWS) and Lower Income Group
(LIG).
      For    the     LIG     category,     TNHB   offers
meticulously designed 2-bedroom flats with a
carpet area of 600 sq. ft. in urban regions and
900 sq. ft. in rural areas. For those in the EWS
category, single-bedroom flats featuring a multi-
purpose hall with a carpet area of 400 sq. ft.
1.2 Organizational Structure
      The Tamil Nadu Housing Board (TNHB)
functions    under     the       Housing    and   Urban
Development Department of the Government of
Tamil Nadu. The organisation is governed by a
Board, headed by a Chairman appointed by the
Government, and other official Board Members.
This cohesive framework lays the foundation for
effective   governance, ensuring strategic decision-
making and optimal resource management.

1.4.1 Area Development Scheme

plots    are        allocated        through     transparent
processes such as e-lots or e-auctions. Post-
completion,      the     developed       infrastructure       is
handed over to local authorities, ensuring long-
term     maintenance        and       upkeep.    TNHB        has
developed 2,31,921 plots all over the state from
its inception.

offices   and   other    facilities     within   walking
distance. These projects also attract business,
further boosting the local economy.

2.4 Regional Plan
      Regional Plan constitutes a specialised
planning framework for a homogeneous area
encompassing two or more districts, aimed at
fostering long-term growth and development
through the integration of economic, social, and
environmental goals. It ensures the efficient
utilisation of resources across the entire region,
thereby      facilitating        balanced        regional
development.
2.4.1 Goals of Regional Planning
  • Balanced       Growth:         Preventing      over-
    concentration     of    development     in     single
    nodes by identifying growth centres, new
    towns and cities within the region, thereby
    promoting balanced regional development.
  • Transport Connectivity: Analysing inter-
    city traffic and transport linkages to create a

                            20
  more efficient network for the movement of
  people and goods between rural and urban
  areas.
• Environmental Protection: Safeguarding
  shared natural resources through equitable
  and sustainable development frameworks.
• Optimal           Resource            Management:
  Organising       economic         activities,      natural
  resources,      and      settlement         patterns    to
  ensure that local resources are utilised
  efficiently    for     enhanced      economic        well-
  being.
• Infrastructure              and            Investment:
  Facilitating          targeted      investment          in
  infrastructure          including         transportation,
  communication,          and      public     services   as
  critical inputs for economic growth.
• Social     Equity:         Prioritising      the     basic
  minimum needs of backward areas and
  ensuring       that     social    services      such   as
  healthcare and education are accessible to

                            21
                            21
      all sections of the population within the
      region.

2.5 Master Plan
          A structured and forward-looking approach
to   urban      development         is   indispensable      for
ensuring planned, sustainable, and inclusive
growth across Tamil Nadu. Master Plans serve as
comprehensive strategic framework that guide
the spatial and economic development of urban
areas over a 20-year planning horizon. They
address         emerging          urbanisation       trends,
infrastructure          imperatives,          environmental


                               23
                               23
sustainability,   and     socio-economic      priorities,
enabling the optimal and efficient utilisation of
land   and   resources.     The    planning     process
incorporates inputs from the public, local bodies,
and line departments to ensure transparency,
inclusiveness,    and     responsiveness      to   local
development needs.
       The DTCP is mandated to prepare and
periodically revise Master Plans, providing a
comprehensive spatial framework for balanced
and equitable regional development. At present,
123    Master     Plans   are     under   preparation,
covering 130 towns under various State and
Central Government schemes. Upon statutory
notification, the proportion of planned urban
areas is projected to increase from 7% to 22%.
Advanced technologies including GIS, Remote
Sensing, UAV surveys, and web-based cloud GIS
platforms are employed throughout the planning
exercise.

2.8 State-wide Single Window Portal — Key

2.8.1 Single Window Portal
      The   Single     Window    Portal     has      been
operationalized to facilitate easy and efficient
processing of planning permissions for layout,

      The     Instant   Approval    through     Self-

3,500 sq.ft. (Ground Floor or Ground + 1 Floor,

                          33
                          33
maximum height 7 metres), without obtaining a
completion certificate. Since its introduction,
more than two lakh beneficiaries have availed its
benefits. The scope has since been expanded to
cover Stilt + 2 Floors up to 10 metres height.

      The       Deemed      NOC    system   prescribes
specific timelines for NOC-issuing departments
to process and issue clearances. The system-
based workflow enables timely scrutiny, online
submission of replies by the applicant and
automatic initiation of deemed approval where
action is not taken within the stipulated period,
significantly    reducing    delays   and   improving
transparency.

      The primary objective of Enforcement Cell

will expand significantly due to increased self-
certification approvals and the introduction of
third-party certification for building approvals.

                           54
To        effectively     curb   violations      and

GIS-based software: As a pilot project in

that uses satellite       imagery and Geographic
Information System (GIS) technology to detect
building violations early. Operating as a unified

constructions and dispatches automated alerts
to district officers to guide targeted enforcement
action.

2.21.3 Land Pooling and Area Development
         Scheme (LPADS)
        The Land Pooling and Area Development
Scheme       (LPADS)         is    a   progressive        land
management and urban development framework
aimed at consolidating fragmented land parcels
into integrated and comprehensively planned
urban      areas.      The        scheme      enables      the
coordinated provision of critical infrastructure
and public facilities, including road networks,
open spaces, parks, and utility services, while
ensuring        a     balanced         and      transparent
redistribution        of    developed        land        among
participating       landowners.        By    adopting      this
approach,       LPADS        promotes        efficient    land

                              57
                              57
utilisation,     planned      urban        expansion,     and
enhanced land value for stakeholders.
       As a preliminary initiative, Hosur has been
identified for the implementation of a pilot
project     to   showcase          the    applicability   and
benefits of LPADS. In this connection, DTCP will
engage a consultant to undertake the project
within the Hosur New Town Development Area.
2.21.4 Land Use Information System (LUIS)
       A centralised Land Use Information Portal
has been proposed to provide seamless public
access      to        land    use        information      from
implemented Master Plans, currently available
only   in      hard    copy    at    respective     planning
authority offices. The portal will serve as a
GIS-based spatial land use information system
integrating planning area boundaries, survey
parcels, land use classification, water bodies,
and road networks, enhancing transparency and
utility for Government departments, investors,
and citizens.

   4. TAMIL NADU URBAN HABITAT
        DEVELOPMENT BOARD

      The Tamil Nadu Slum Clearance Board was
established in the year 1970 to provide social
housing with infrastructure for the urban poor in
Chennai and later extended to other urban areas
of Tamil Nadu. The nomenclature of Tamil Nadu
Slum Clearance Board has been changed to
Tamil Nadu Urban Habitat Development Board in
the year 2021. TNUHDB is governed by the
Tamil Nadu Slum Areas (Improvement and
Clearance) Act, 1971 and is functioning under
the control of a Chairman, Managing Director
and Joint Managing Director.
      Tamil     Nadu       has     experienced     rapid
urbanization    in   the    past    decades      due   to
technical     advancement,         industrial    growth,
economic diversification and rural to urban
migration.     The   concentration       of     economic
activities in the urban areas attracted millions of

                           94
                           94
people to migrate to cities in search of better
employment, education and living standards.
Due to growth in the manufacturing sector,
service sector and surge of the knowledge
economy, the population pressure on cities
escalates.    As   a    result,      the     urban    centres
increasingly are susceptible to the negative
consequences of rapid urbanization, such as
mushrooming of unplanned and substandard
habitations, congested settlements, shortage of
housing      and       basic        amenities,       poverty,
unemployment       etc.    Such        settlements        later
become slums. The proliferation of slums often
accompanies rapid urbanization and poses great
social and policy challenges.

      Urban    slums      lack       basic    infrastructure
which lead to poor living conditions and health
hazards, overcrowding and substandard housing
structures    make      slums       vulnerable       to   fires,
natural disasters, and health hazards. Social


                               95
                               95
challenges, such as poverty, unemployment,
and limited access to education and healthcare,
perpetuate     cycles    of deprivation.    Therefore,
making Tamil Nadu slum free and provision of
affordable    housing     are   the   key    areas     of
intervention for the Government.

4.1 Slum Free Cities

       The Government emphasizes the provision
of   formal    houses,    infrastructures    and     land
tenure to all the urban slum families to attain
the vision of slum free cities before 2031.

       In alignment with this vision, the Tamil
Nadu    Urban    Habitat    Development      Board     is
committed to providing adequate housing with
infrastructure to all urban poor families to
achieve slum-free urban Tamil Nadu by 2031.
TNUHDB conducted housing demand survey in
2014 and identified that 9.08 lakh families living
in the urban areas of Tamil Nadu required
housing.      Against    this   target,     4.68     lakh

                           96
tenement/houses have been constructed till
date,     72,976     tenements/houses         are     under
various     stages    of      progress     and      will   be
completed before 2027.
        A comprehensive survey will be conducted
during 2026-27 to reassess the housing demand
among urban slum and poor families, enabling
the     formulation      of      targeted,    time-bound
schemes so as to achieve the targets within the
time frame.
4.2 Social housing with infrastructure for
       the urban poor – Approaches

        The following multi-pronged approaches
are adopted to achieve the goal of resilient, safe
tenements and quality infrastructure for all
urban slum families by 2031.

4.16 New Strategies
       The following strategies are proposed to
be followed to support the vision of ensuring a
slum free Tamil Nadu by 2031 by providing
tenements and houses to all the families.
a) Strategic Framework Development
       A comprehensive assessment of urban
poor housing requirement will be undertaken,
analyzing      existing     conditions,   informal
settlements, rental patterns, tenure security,
and access to adequate shelter. Socio-economic
factors such as affordability, income levels,
employment trends, and household structures
will be studied for targeted interventions. Future
demand is to be projected based on population

                          129
                          129
growth,     migration,    and   economic       shifts,
identifying key pressure points so as to frame
the strategies and approaches to achieve the
Vision of slum free Tamil Nadu by 2031.
b) Effective Utilization of Land Assets for
   Housing Development
      The      Board’s   land    assets    will     be
systematically       mapped,    categorized,       and
optimized for efficient utilization. Digitization of
land records will be undertaken for efficient land
management.
c) Maximizing FSI Potential to Improve
   Housing Supply for the Urban Poor
      The maximum permissible Floor Space
Index (FSI) will be determined and utilized to
optimize land use and maximize housing supply,
particularly    in   high-demand     areas,       while
ensuring adequate infrastructure.




                         130
d) Urban Design Integration for Enhanced
   Livability
       Urban design principles will be integrated
into the projects to enhance the overall quality
of life, climate resilience, sustainability, safety
and    inclusion.   Adequate,      well-designed       and
appropriately       located     social       infrastructure
including open spaces and community facilities
will   be     incorporated       to        foster   livable

      To promote and regulate the real estate
sector efficiently and transparently, the Real
Estate (Regulation & Development) Act of 2016
was enacted, which safeguards the interests of
buyers of plots, residential flats and commercial
buildings. To enforce this Act in Tamil Nadu, the
State Government introduced the Tamil Nadu
Real Estate (Regulation & Development) Rules in
2017 and established the Tamil Nadu Real Estate
Regulatory Authority (TNRERA), which functions
with a Chairperson and two Members.
  •   The key responsibilities of TNRERA include
registering real estate projects and agents, as
well as addressing complaints. Projects involving
sales (whether freehold or leasehold), where the
land exceeds 500 square meters and includes
more than eight apartments (across all phases),
must be registered with TNRERA.
